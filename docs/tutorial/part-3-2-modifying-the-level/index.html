Tutorial: Build a Platformer Game

Part 3-2: The level

What you will learn: how to load a level, and how to change it.

This is the part where the grey screen turns into a game.

Step 1: list the files

You copied map1.tmx into the project in Part 2. melonJS will not touch it until you list it, so open src/manifest.ts and add these lines to the array. Watch the comma: the last entry that is already there does not have one.

    },

    /* Level */
    { name: "tileset",    type: "image", src: "/data/img/tileset.png" },
    { name: "background", type: "image", src: "/data/img/background.png" },
    { name: "clouds",     type: "image", src: "/data/img/clouds.png" },
    { name: "tileset",    type: "tsx",   src: "/data/map/tileset.tsx" },
    { name: "map1",       type: "tmx",   src: "/data/map/map1.tmx" }
];

Five lines for one level, because a level is made of several files:

  • the tileset image, the grid of squares the level is drawn with
  • the background and clouds, which scroll behind at their own speed
  • the tileset description, which says how to cut that image into squares
  • the map itself, which says which square goes where

Miss one and melonJS tells you exactly which name it could not find.

Step 2: load the level

Open src/scripts/stage/play.ts. Delete what is inside onResetEvent and put this in its place:

onResetEvent() {
    level.load("map1");
}

Change the import at the top of the file to bring in level instead of ColorLayer and BitmapText. Save.

You should see: the level in your browser, instead of Hello World!.

One line loaded a whole level. That is the payoff for listing the files in step 1.

You will also see an error in the browser console, about a texture that is not found. That is expected. The map contains your character, and the starter does not know how to draw it yet. Part 3-3 is where you fix that.

Step 3: change it

Open src/data/map/map1.tmx in Tiled. The level is in the middle. On the right you have your layers at the top, and the tiles you paint with underneath.

Layers are the thing to understand. A level is stacked: sky at the back, ground in the middle, decoration in front. Check which layer is selected before you paint, or your mushrooms end up behind the hill instead of in front of it. The eye icon hides a layer, which is the fastest way to see what is on it.

The black outlines are collision: the floors and walls your character cannot pass through. They sit on their own layer, and melonJS turns them into physics when the level loads. If your character falls through the floor later, look here first.

Paint a few tiles, then save with File > Save.

You should see: your change in the browser, straight away.

Tiled can do far more than this. Its documentation is good, and the melonJS wiki explains how collision shapes work.
