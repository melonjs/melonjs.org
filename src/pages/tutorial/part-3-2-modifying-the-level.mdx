## Part 3-2: The level

**What you will learn:** how to load a level, and how to change it.

This is the part where the grey screen turns into a game.

## Step 1: list the files

You copied `map1.tmx` into the project in Part 2. melonJS will not touch it
until you list it, so open `src/manifest.ts` and add these lines to the array.
Watch the comma: the last entry that is already there does not have one.

```ts
    },

    /* Level */
    { name: "tileset",    type: "image", src: "/data/img/tileset.png" },
    { name: "background", type: "image", src: "/data/img/background.png" },
    { name: "clouds",     type: "image", src: "/data/img/clouds.png" },
    { name: "tileset",    type: "tsx",   src: "/data/map/tileset.tsx" },
    { name: "map1",       type: "tmx",   src: "/data/map/map1.tmx" }
];
```

Five lines for one level, because a level is made of several files:

- the **tileset image**, the grid of squares the level is drawn with
- the **background** and **clouds**, which scroll behind at their own speed
- the **tileset** description, which says how to cut that image into squares
- the **map** itself, which says which square goes where

Miss one and melonJS tells you exactly which name it could not find.

## Step 2: load the level

Open `src/scripts/stage/play.ts`. Delete what is inside `onResetEvent` and put
this in its place:

```ts
onResetEvent() {
    level.load("map1");
}
```

Change the import at the top of the file to bring in `level` instead of
`ColorLayer` and `BitmapText`. Save.

**You should see:** a black screen, and an error in the browser console about a
texture that is not found.

That is expected, and worth understanding before you move on. Your one line did
load the whole level, which is the payoff for listing the files in step 1. But
the map also places your character in it, and the starter does not know how to
draw it yet, so loading stops on that error before anything reaches the screen.
Part 3-3 fixes it, and the level appears along with it.

## Step 3: change it

Open `src/data/map/map1.tmx` in Tiled. The level is in the middle. On the right
you have your layers at the top, and the tiles you paint with underneath.

<Image alt="Tiled editor screenshot" class="large" src={tiledEditor} />

**Layers are the thing to understand.** A level is stacked: sky at the back,
ground in the middle, decoration in front. Check which layer is selected before
you paint, or your mushrooms end up behind the hill instead of in front of it.
The eye icon hides a layer, which is the fastest way to see what is on it.

<Image alt="Putting mushrooms in the sky" class="large" src={mushroomSky} />

The black outlines are **collision**: the floors and walls your character cannot
pass through. They sit on their own layer, and melonJS turns them into physics
when the level loads. If your character falls through the floor later, look here
first.

Paint a few tiles, then save with `File > Save`.

**You should see:** your change in the browser, straight away.

Tiled can do far more than this. Its
[documentation](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/) is good, and the melonJS
wiki explains
[how collision shapes work](https://github.com/melonjs/melonJS/wiki/How-to-define-world-collision-shapes-in-Tiled).
