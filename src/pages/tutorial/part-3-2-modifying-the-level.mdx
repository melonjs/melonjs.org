## Part 3-2: The level

This is the part where the grey screen becomes a game.

## Load the map

Your project already has `map1.tmx` in `src/data/map` from Part 2. melonJS will
not load it until you list it, so open `src/manifest.ts` and add three entries:

```
{ name: "tileset", type: "image", src: "/src/data/img/tileset.png" },
{ name: "tileset", type: "tsx",   src: "/src/data/map/tileset.tsx" },
{ name: "map1",    type: "tmx",   src: "/src/data/map/map1.tmx" },
```

Three entries for one level, because a level is three things: the picture of the
tiles, the description of how that picture is cut into tiles, and the map that
arranges them. The two `tileset` entries share a name on purpose; melonJS looks
them up together.

Now open `src/scripts/stage/play.ts` and replace the "Hello World" body of
`onResetEvent` with one line:

```
onResetEvent() {
    level.load("map1");
}
```

Import `level` from `melonjs` at the top of the file alongside `Stage`. Save,
and your browser now shows the platformer level.

## Edit it

Open `src/data/map/map1.tmx` in Tiled. The middle is the level. On the right are
the layers at the top, and the tiles you can paint with at the bottom.

Layers are the thing to understand. A level is stacked: background art behind,
the ground and platforms you stand on, decorations in front. Always check which
layer is selected before you paint, or your mushrooms end up behind the hill
instead of on it. The eye icon next to each layer hides it, which is the quickest
way to see what belongs to what.

<Image alt="Putting mushrooms in the sky" class="large" src={mushroomSky} />

The solid black lines in the level are collision shapes: the walls, floors and
boxes your character cannot walk through. They live on their own layer, and
melonJS turns them into physics when the level loads. If your character falls
through the floor later, this layer is the first place to look.

When you are done, save with `File > Save`. The dev server reloads and your
change is in the game.

_A note on formats: melonJS reads `.tmx` directly, so saving is enough. Tiled can
also export to JSON via `File > Export As` if you prefer that, in which case
change the manifest entry to point at the `.json` file._

You can learn much more about Tiled in
[its own documentation](https://doc.mapeditor.org/en/stable/), and about
[how collision shapes are defined](https://github.com/melonjs/melonJS/wiki/How-to-define-world-collision-shapes-in-Tiled)
in the melonJS wiki.
