## Part 3-1: The art

**What you will learn:** the two kinds of image a game uses. This part is
optional. Your project already has both, so you can go straight to
[the level](/tutorial/part-3-2-modifying-the-level) and come back later.

## Tilesets

A **tileset** is a grid of small squares: grass, dirt, a crate, a mushroom. Your
level is built by saying "square 14 goes here", which is why a whole level is a
tiny file. Yours is `src/data/img/tileset.png`.

## Sprite atlases

An **atlas** is one image holding every frame of an animation, plus a `.json`
file saying where each frame sits inside it. Yours is `texture.png` with
`texture.json` beside it. The walk cycle lives in there as frames named
`walk0001.png` and up.

## Drawing your own

[Aseprite](https://www.aseprite.org) is the usual choice, and melonJS reads its
files directly: save an `.aseprite` file into `src/data/img` and the frame tags
you used to name your animations come across with it, with no export step. It
costs about twenty dollars, or nothing if you compile it yourself.
[LibreSprite](https://libresprite.github.io) is a free fork.

Whatever you use, **draw on a new layer** rather than on top of the original.
Then you can hide it, erase it, or start again without having lost anything.

{/* SCREENSHOT: a tileset open in Aseprite with a new layer added on top */}

One rule: keep new frames the same size as the old ones. Change the size and the
atlas no longer matches the image, so the animation plays the wrong rectangles.
